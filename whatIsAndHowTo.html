What is DV6

Division Six is a Table Top Roleplaying Game (TTRPG) inspired by everything we've ever seen: movies, shows, comics, anime, books, and most other forms of entertainment.

To allow players to create the character they have always wanted to play and mold said character in their own way, for the characters entire life. Like other popular TTRPGs, DV6 is a D20 system, meaning your twenty sided dice is used to perform almost anything in the game, in and out of combat.

If you've played other table top D20 systems before the transition to DV6 should be relatively painless and easy.

Note: DV6 core game is designed to be 100% compatibility with all campaign settings and modules of other D20 RPG’s.

What is a TTRPG

A TTRPG is a pencil, paper and dice game, where you and your group of friends get together, sit around a table and use your imagination to solve riddles & puzzles, navigate dungeons, uncover the mysteries, fight the bad guys, try to save the world.

Table Top Role Playing Games (TTRPG) are different from any game you ever played. They are 100% about your imagination and require a higher level of dedication than most other game types. However in the same note it also gives you a greater level of control and influence over the story and your character than any other type of game.

The 2 major roles in a TTRPG are the Game Master (GM) and the Player Characters (PC).

Game Master (GM)

This role is the most important and dedicated role in the game. As the GM it is your job alone to be the game itself. You and your computer (and/or papers) are responsible for the following:

  1. Have a campaign setting and story ready for the party to play.
  2. Have major story NPC's planned and be ready to role play each of them.
  3. Have Quests and Quest-givers planned for both Major Story Arcs & Side Quests.
  4. Have Maps of the land, region, continent, state or wherever the adventure will take place accessible.
    You will also need many specific scene maps, such as caves, dungeons, important buildings the party will be adventuring in.
  5. You must be descriptive: always be ready to explain the area and answer questions from the players when they are searching / investigating something.
  6. You are the rule keeper: it's up to you to enforce all the rules and keep the game flowing nicely.
  7. You are also there to keep the players in character, to tell them when they are acting contrary to their character.
  8. The GM also assigns all difficulty numbers for challenges the Players will attempt and encounter.

The GM doesn't just tell a story, he or she creates a foundation and skeleton of a adventure, then the players assume the roles of the main characters in the story. Their actions create the filler and determine the outcome of the story's events.

Play focus: While role playing you will need to have all other forms of visual distraction turned off. When accessing your imagination visual distractions cause you to lose focus, causing you to lose the sense of imagining yourself in another world, playing a character. So put your Devices away and get into character. Turn on some cool music for background noise. See the GM’s Book for more information about this role.

Player Characters (PC)

As the players it’s your job to create Characters that form a Party, and undertake a perilous journey full of intrigue, traps, monsters and damsels in distress. The following is a list to help you learn how to play your character correctly.

  1. Getting into Character: The single most important thing all players must practice is playing and reacting as your character, not as yourself, and responding appropriately to the situation happening in the game. Far too often new players tend to think of their characters as disposable jerks who can do the dumbest stuff and disrupt the game. Acting in character as much as possible will help other players stay in character.
  2. Main Traits: These are the four personality traits that best describe the way your character acts and responds to situations. Every character will have four Main Traits: one from their class, two chosen by the player, and the last assigned by the GM. So think about how you want your character to act and choose appropriately. Try not to choose traits that you don't want to play. The fourth trait is given to you by the GM, so you're stuck playing that one — all the rest are yours to choose.
  3. Player Knowledge vs Character Knowledge: Player Knowledge and Character Knowledge are two different things, and you are not allowed to use Player knowledge to help your character.
    Example: If your character does not have Drive skill they cannot drive a car, no matter how much you know about driving a car and how simple an act it is. Your character has never driven and so cannot simply drive the car.
  4. Questions: You play the game by asking the GM questions. The game happens around a table with all the players interacting with each other as their characters. However, everything else in the game must be done through the GM and may require your character win challenges or rolls to succeed.

    Even though you must remain in character for all in-game situations, asking the GM a question is done as a player (out of character). Yet some questions are interchangeable, in these cases you need to signify who you are asking.

    Example: You wish to know the dimensions of the room you are in. This could be asked out loud to the rest of the party or perhaps whispered to another party member, or it could be asked out of character to the GM.
